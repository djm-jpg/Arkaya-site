A worked example

One payment, two sets of records.

A fictional company lets an AI agent pay supplier invoices. It is told to pay GBP 50,000 to one supplier. The money goes to another.

Illustrative, synthetic data; not any insurer's requirement

With every record

  • The instruction names Supplier-Pebble-11.
  • A human checker approves Supplier-Pebble-11.
  • The agent reads the invoice: Supplier-Pebble-11.
  • The agent pays Supplier-Cobalt-22. The per-transaction limit check passes.
  • The ledger posts GBP 50,000 to Supplier-Cobalt-22. An exception opens.

The records show where the switch happened.

Illustrative, synthetic data; not any insurer's requirement

With the agent's log missing

  • The instruction names Supplier-Pebble-11.
  • A human checker approves Supplier-Pebble-11.
  • The agent's log: absent.
  • What the agent did: not recorded.
  • The ledger posts GBP 50,000 to Supplier-Cobalt-22. An exception opens.

The payment record is intact. It cannot show how the switch happened, or which model made the payment.

"With every record" means no required source field is missing in this scenario, not that any insurer's own requirements are met. Times are as the records declare them and are not anchored outside the source system.

The difference is the missing log, not the way the records are laid out.

Why it matters

After a loss, what can be shown depends on what was recorded.

When an insurer pays an AI loss, it may look to recover from the vendor whose component failed. That depends on evidence of what happened.

Some provider records can be retrieved for only days. One agent-session API returns sessions that started in the previous 72 hours [1]; one enterprise compliance log platform keeps data for 30 days [2]. Unless the record is exported and kept, the question may not be answerable.

[1] Salesforce, Agentforce Session Trace OTel API (beta): “The API returns data for sessions that started (StartTimestamp) within the previous 72 hours of the API request.” [2] OpenAI, Compliance Platform for Enterprise and Edu customers: “The Compliance Logs Platform retains data for 30 days.” Both describe availability from the provider, not whether copies were exported and kept.
