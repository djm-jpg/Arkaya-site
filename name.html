Why the name Arkaya?

Better evidence can show that things which look alike are different.

Our name comes from archaea, microbes that were long grouped with bacteria because, under the microscope, they looked much the same.

In 1977, Carl Woese and George Fox compared ribosomal RNA from a range of microbes. Their comparison set one group, methane-producing microbes they called archaebacteria, apart from the bacteria as a separate line of descent.[1] In 1990, Woese, Otto Kandler and Mark Wheelis proposed the name Archaea for that line, as one of three domains of life alongside Bacteria and Eucarya.[2]

The organisms had always been there. A new way of reading the evidence showed a distinction the existing classification had missed. Later genome studies have revised the picture again, placing complex cells, the kind that make up plants and animals, within the Archaea rather than alongside them.[3] The classification moved twice because the evidence did.

How the idea applies to Arkaya

An organisation can describe its policies, approvals and oversight. An insurer, lender or acquirer looks for evidence of what happened: who had authority, which controls operated, what changed and whether remediation worked.

Two organisations can describe their governance in the same words and carry very different risks. The difference matters more as AI systems take on decisions that people used to make.

Arkaya stewards an open schema, the Governance Evidence Taxonomy, which gives governance records a shared structure. When an insurer, lender or acquirer appoints us, we read the records a company supplies against that reader's question. We report what the records support, what they do not show, and the limits of our findings.

The insurer, lender or acquirer decides whether that evidence is sufficient for its decision, and sets the terms and the price. Arkaya prices nothing.

What we take from the comparison is a method, not a conclusion. A biological classification says nothing about how any organisation should be insured, financed or valued. It does show what close reading of evidence can do: test familiar categories, and change the conclusion when the evidence requires it.

Compared catalogues of ribosomal RNA fragments across microbes and argued that life comprises three primary lines of descent, one of them the archaebacteria. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences USA 87(12), 4576–4579. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.87.12.4576. Proposed the domain as a new rank above kingdom and gave the three domains their names. This is the 1990 naming, thirteen years after the finding it formalised.
  3. Eme, L., Tamarit, D., Caceres, E. F. and others (2023). Inference and reconstruction of the heimdallarchaeial ancestry of eukaryotes. Nature 618, 992–999. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06186-2. Places eukaryotes, the group that includes plants and animals, within the Asgard archaea. The source for the statement that later evidence revised the three-domain picture.
